### Ordinary lesson checks

1. **Prompt:** “Tutor-created scenario: Your team enables 10% mirroring to green, then runs all its test requests with --deployment-name blue. Green’s logs show no requests from those tests. What would you change to let those tests exercise the mirroring configuration while keeping blue responsible for the client-facing predictions?” **Initial learner:** “remove deployment-name, so it mirrors 10% of the traffic”. **Feedback:** correct: ordinary routing to blue and eligible 10% request copies; percentage does not guarantee a particular count in a small batch.

2. **Prompt:** “Application check: Green is now serving 10% of live traffic, but its errors rise sharply. Blue remains healthy and has enough capacity. What would you change first—and why would you avoid deleting blue during the rollout?” **Initial learner:** “I will change the traffic to blue=100.” **Feedback:** correct rollback routing; tutor supplied green=0 and the omitted reason to retain blue: a running ready rollback target avoids rebuilding. Do not attribute that rationale to the learner.

3. **Prompt:** “Quick decision: Green has just reached 100% live traffic and looks healthy for five minutes. A teammate suggests deleting blue immediately to save money. Would you delete it now or retain it for a validation window—and what trade-off drives your choice?” **Initial learner:** “retain it for now for validation window. Unfortunately, having it live, even without traffic, will cost compute.” **Feedback:** correct cost/readiness tradeoff; clarified blue is deployed and available, not serving ordinary live traffic.

4. **Prompt:** “Decision check: After promoting green, you reduce blue’s instance count substantially. Later, you need to restore blue to 100% traffic. What risk does that introduce, even though the routing change is still possible?” **Initial learner:** “Instances is where the thing runs. If you reduce the instances, it might slow down how predictions process because of instance availability.” **Feedback:** essentially correct capacity risk; tutor refined it to less concurrency, contention/queueing/latency/timeouts or rejection, not necessarily slower individual computation.

5. **Prompt:** “Tutor-created decision check: You want more shadow-test coverage, but the endpoint is already close to its bandwidth quota. Would increasing mirroring from 10% to 50% be risk-free? What would you check first?” **Initial learner:** “Having more traffic routed means you might require more instances to handle the load/capacity. So it will cost some compute.” **Feedback:** compute concern acknowledged, but bandwidth quota constraint missing; tutor clarified mirrored copies are not live routing, extra instances do not increase bandwidth quota, and supplied headroom/throttling checks.

6. **Follow-up prompt:** “One quick application: If bandwidth—not compute—is the bottleneck, would adding green instances alone solve it, or would you keep mirroring lower while addressing the bandwidth limit?” **Learner:** “No. We need to address the bandwidth.” **Feedback:** correct scaffolded confirmation, not independent full diagnosis.

7. After commands for mirror green=0, live blue=90 green=10, then blue=0 green=100: **Prompt:** “Quick command-reading check: Which line first allows ordinary clients to receive green’s predictions—and why doesn’t the preceding line do that?” **Initial learner:** “--mirror-traffic first, so it will receive traffic without affecting user. Alternatively, set the --traffic with a value for green to route some traffic to that endpoint.” **Feedback:** general distinction partly present, but literal mirror green=0 disables shadowing; tutor supplied that `--traffic blue=90 green=10` first enables client-facing promotion, and corrected green to a deployment, not a separate endpoint.

8. **Follow-up prompt:** “Quick confirmation: What does --mirror-traffic "green=10" do differently from --traffic "blue=90 green=10"?” **Learner:** “--mirror-traffic just sends shadowed request to green, while the --traffic sets the routing of the message for 90 to blue and 10 to green”. **Feedback:** correct shadow/live distinction; tutor reinforced client response and required mirror removal. Scaffolded recovery did not independently recheck the zero literal.

9. **Prompt:** “Last application check: Green is serving 100% of traffic and has passed validation. You want to stop paying for blue while keeping the service available. Which resource would you delete?” **Initial learner:** “the blue endpoint”. **Feedback:** incorrect resource term; tutor showed one endpoint with blue/green deployments and explained deleting the blue deployment preserves the service, whereas endpoint deletion removes the service and deployments.

10. **Follow-up prompt:** “Quick confirmation: To retire blue while keeping green available, would you use az ml online-deployment delete or az ml online-endpoint delete?” **Learner:** “online-deployment”. **Feedback:** correct scaffolded binary confirmation; no independent transfer after correction.
