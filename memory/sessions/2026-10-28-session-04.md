### Ordinary lesson checks preserved from notes

**M01-U02**

- Original tutor-created prompt: 200 transactions, 20 actual frauds, 30 flagged, 15 correctly flagged; calculate precision/recall and decide against at least 80% fraud recall. Actual response: "Precision is 10% Recall is 15/30 is the recall. So no, it does not meet the reuqirements". Feedback: decision correct but calculations incorrect; precision 15/30 = 50%, recall 15/20 = 75%, so recall fails. Total transactions is neither denominator.
- Follow-up tutor-created prompt: 10 actual frauds, 20 flagged, 8 correctly flagged; calculate both and compare with at least 80% recall. Learner first said "Oh, I just misread the original question". After repeating the smaller prompt, actual response: "Recall is 75% Precision is at 10%, and 75% is lower than 80% so it is a fail". Feedback: appeared to mix examples; 75% belonged to the original scenario. Asked only whether recall's denominator is 10 or 20 and why.
- Actual denominator response: "8/10 10 is actual". Feedback: correct; tutor supplied 80% and explained that equality meets at least 80%. This is not independent percentage/acceptance evidence.
- Precision prompt explicitly supplied 20 flagged and 8 actual frauds among them. Actual response: "8/20 = 40% is my math correct?". Feedback: fraction and arithmetic correct; 40% precision versus 80% recall; recall criterion met.
- Conceptual prompt: fewer false alarms among flagged transactions—precision or recall and why? Actual response: "Precision, because it is related to the flagged trasnactions". Feedback: correct selection and population; higher precision means a smaller false-positive share among flags.

**M01-U03**

- Prompt: sensor sends several JSON readings per minute, model needs one row per machine-minute; is copying files enough and why? Actual response: "No, we need to reduce the unnecessary columns, and do some data cleaning." Feedback: no is correct and cleaning can help, but key transformation is conversion to tabular form, grouping by machine/minute, and averaging readings.
- Prompt: valid readings 18°C, 21°C, 24°C within one machine-minute; what average and one row or three? Actual response: "we want the mean value for all the temps, and store that in one row based on the reuqirements". Feedback: correct operation and granularity; tutor supplied (18 + 21 + 24)/3 = 21°C. Numerical calculation not independently answered.
- Prompt: extract JSON, convert to per-minute averages, then load prepared table to storage: ETL or ELT and why? Actual response: "ETL, because you transformed it before you loaded in the storage." Feedback: correct order and explanation.
- Prompt: retrain every morning using previous day's sensor data; manual or scheduled ingestion and what must be ready before training? Actual response: "scheduled pipeline, give you have an \"every morning\" keyword". Feedback: correct scheduling decision because of recurring preparation; tutor supplied prepared-table readiness, which learner had not answered.
- Tutor-extension prompt: if preparation fails, train on whatever files are present or wait, and why? Actual response: "I am lenient to waiting, because we don't know what is broken in the data." Feedback: sensible wait/investigate decision under uncertain readiness; failure is not proof data itself is broken. A deliberately designed older validated dataset fallback differs from arbitrary-file training.
