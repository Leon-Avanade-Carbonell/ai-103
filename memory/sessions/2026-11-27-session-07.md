### Ordinary lesson checks

1. **Prompt:** 'Tutor-created scenario: A customer asks a support agent to cancel an order. The agent accurately explains the cancellation policy using the supplied documentation, but never attempts cancellation or explains what prevents it. The answer scores highly for relevance and groundedness. Which additional evaluation dimension would best expose the problem, and why?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'It would also need to check for user intent. In this case, the user wanted to help cancel an order, and that was not actioned on.'

   **Feedback:** Correct: intent resolution concerns the fulfilled task. The tutor added that inability should be explained with next steps without claiming success. Task-versus-topic distinction was independent.

2. **Prompt:** 'Tutor-created scenario: Human reviewers frequently disagree about whether a hiking recommendation contains enough safety information. The automated evaluator also disagrees with their scores. A teammate suggests immediately building a custom evaluator. What would you do first, and why?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'Check the discrepancy on the human and automated scores. See if   the rubric is clear and same between all the users.'

   **Feedback:** Affirmed the shared, clear rubric and discrepancy inspection. The tutor emphasized checking human–human consistency first, calibrating and rerating before automation tuning. The answer was relevant but the ordering was not independently explicit.

3. **Prompt:** 'Tutor-created scenario: Reviewers now agree on the rubric. Your custom evaluator gives high scores whenever an answer mentions “weather” and “emergency,” but experts reject some of those answers because the advice is unsafe. Would you trust this evaluator as a deployment gate? What would you change, and how would you verify the change worked?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'No, I will add some safety and risk evaluators.'

   **Feedback:** Correct rejection and safety/risk direction, but custom-logic refinement and validation were incomplete. The tutor supplied that general content safety may miss domain hazards and that the evaluator must assess the advice, not merely keywords.

   **Follow-up prompt:** 'After making those changes, how would you check that the revised evaluators agree with the experts before using them for deployment decisions?'

   **Actual follow-up response:** 'Try to check the human and automated correlation scores.'

   **Feedback:** Yes: repeat shadow rating on shared examples and compare scores. The tutor added inspecting unsafe cases and gate decisions because correlation can hide systematic differences. This is prompted/scaffolded recovery, not independent complete custom-evaluator design or demonstrated understanding of correlation limitations.
