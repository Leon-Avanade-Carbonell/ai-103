### Ordinary lesson checks

1. **Prompt:** 'Tutor-created scenario: You change a support agent’s prompt. Its answers now sound friendlier, and three manual tests look good. Why would you still run an automated evaluation batch before releasing it, and what role would human reviewers retain?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'Yes, it is necessary to run automated evaluation. Himans would check it for the human-ness in the response. To check it does not come off as robotic.'

   **Feedback:** Naturalness is a valid human criterion, but human reviewers also consider usefulness, correctness, safety and suitability and validate automated scores. The missing automated-batch rationale is regression detection across many requests; three good examples are insufficient. Those reasons were tutor-supplied, not independently explained by the learner.

2. **Scaffolded follow-up prompt:** 'Tutor example: The friendlier prompt improves greetings but makes the agent invent refund exceptions. What would you want the evaluation batch to check before approving that prompt—not just whether it sounds friendly?'

   **Actual learner response:** 'I would check for Groundedness and Relevance to the query, We want to make sure it is answering the question correctly with the correct sources and references; as well as a grounded response with the correct facts.'

   **Feedback:** Good choices: groundedness means support by supplied information; relevance means addressing the request. Relevant answers can invent exceptions; citations alone do not prove support. The learner independently selected appropriate criteria after the tutor supplied the regression scenario. This scaffolded application demonstrates the introductory quality idea, not an independent explanation of scale or the broader human role.
