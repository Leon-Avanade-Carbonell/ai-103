### Ordinary lesson checks

1. **Prompt:** 'Your automated evaluator gives a hiking recommendation a high score because it is clear and relevant. A trail expert notices it recommends an exposed ridge during a thunderstorm. What would you change in the evaluation process before trusting it to approve future prompt updates? Plain language is fine—no metric calculations needed.'

   **Initial learner response:** 'a human in the loop would be great to check if the review is correct/safe'

   **Feedback:** Affirmed human oversight catches safety misses. The tutor supplied the further steps: compare human and automated judgments, investigate missing weather-safety criteria, refine and revalidate. Those steps were not independently demonstrated by the learner.

2. **Prompt after teaching:** 'One practical choice: after validation, would you have humans review only low-scoring answers, or low-scoring answers plus a random sample of high-scoring ones? Why?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'low-scoring and random sample of high-scoring ones, just so we have a bit of broad samples to work with, and not miss out on possible false positives (and negatives)'

   **Feedback:** Correct breadth and blind-spot rationale. Low-score review can find genuine problems and false rejection; high-score sampling catches false approval. The tutor clarified that positive/negative depend on what positive denotes and recommended explicit approval/rejection names. This was a supported application following explanation, not an independent full process-design demonstration. No retry or further response reported.
