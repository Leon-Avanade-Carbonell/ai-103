### Ordinary lesson checks

1. **Prompt:** 'Tutor-created scenario: a refund-support agent’s evaluation dataset contains 500 genuine customer questions, but almost all are straightforward requests within the refund window. Would you trust a high average evaluation score enough to release a new prompt? What two kinds of cases would you add, and why?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'It should cover more cases than the high and average evaluation score. I would add some variation and adversarial cases'

   **Feedback:** Accepted the coverage concern and case choices. The tutor supplied examples of different refund wording, policy bypass and an edge case exactly at the deadline. The learner did not independently explain why or construct examples.

2. **Prompt:** 'You’re preparing refund-agent tests from real support tickets containing names, email addresses, purchase dates, and policy details. What would you remove or replace, and what information would you preserve so the tests still check refund eligibility?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'I will try to remove the names and emails, and try to replace them with some unique ID in my datatbase. keep everything else'

   **Feedback:** Recognized direct-identifier removal and retention of decision facts. The tutor explained that database-linked IDs are pseudonymization, suggested test-only IDs when possible, reviewing remaining and free-text fields for PII, and using relative dates to preserve eligibility. These refinements were tutor-supplied; no follow-up privacy check occurred.

3. **Context:** After an illustrative JSON example with query refund at 35 days, context refunds within 30 days with no exceptions, ground_truth outside the window and response refunds within 60 days.

   **Prompt:** 'For the example above, which field should a groundedness evaluator use as its supporting evidence—and why wouldn’t checking relevance alone catch the problem?'

   **Initial learner response:** 'Query, context, response, and ground_truth (because it is available)'

   **Feedback:** The tutor distinguished context as supporting evidence, query as request, response as the judged answer and optional ground_truth as a reference; the answer is relevant but contradicts context. The initial response did not isolate supporting evidence or explain relevance.

   **Scaffolded follow-up prompt:** 'if we removed ground_truth but kept the query, response, and policy context, could we still identify that groundedness failure? Why?'

   **Actual follow-up response:** 'The context contains the requirement of the 30 days of purchase. So yes.'

   **Feedback:** Correct: the policy context identifies the unsupported 60-day claim. Feedback was given via the quiz-offer question. This demonstrates context use after instruction, not an independent replacement for the initial answer.
