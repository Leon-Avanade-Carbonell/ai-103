### Q02 — Reporting intervals and per-trial delay

[Persisted Q02](../../lessons/01-mlops/04-hyperparameter-tuning/quizzes.md#quiz-2026-11-06-session-03-q02) · medium · multiple-answer · M04-U04 · S1.

Prompt: A trial logs its primary metric twice per epoch. Which statements correctly describe early-termination timing? Select two answers.
- A. Each epoch counts once — An epoch is always one evaluation interval.
- B. Each metric report counts — Each primary-metric log is a reporting interval.
- C. Delay counts finished trials — Evaluation waits for other trials to finish.
- D. Delay is per trial — Each trial receives its own initial reporting period.

Initial D, A (returned order), set A,D; key B,D; incorrect exact set. Explanation: D was correctly selected, but B was omitted and A incorrectly selected. Primary-metric reports count as intervals, so two logs per epoch produce two reporting intervals. Delay is per trial, not based on completed trials (C).

**Post-quiz follow-up, one sequence, not independent successes:** Prompt: “If a trial logs its primary metric twice per epoch, how many reporting intervals has it produced after two epochs?” Response: “it will have logged 4 primary metrics over two intervals.” Feedback: four reports but four reporting intervals across two epochs. Tutor supplied the table: epoch 1 reports 1 and 2, epoch 2 reports 3 and 4.

Prompt: “So with delay_evaluation=4, has this trial supplied the four reporting intervals needed for the initial delay after those two epochs?” Response: “epochs is when we do evaluations, right? I assume that delay_evaluation is in epochs. So it will need two more epochs before the logic is applied?” Feedback: incorrect; an epoch is a pass through the dataset, and the script controls metric evaluation and logging. Delay counts reports, not epochs. Tutor acknowledged the earlier once-per-epoch illustration may have blurred the distinction and supplied a comparison: once per epoch requires four epochs for four reports; twice per epoch requires two. Two epochs already supply four reports, no two additional epochs are needed; the source's first stopping check for delay 4 and interval 1 is at the fifth reporting interval.

Final scaffolded prompt: “A trial has logged its primary metric only three times. Has it supplied the four reporting intervals required by delay_evaluation=4 yet?” Response: “no. you need one more log report of the primary metric”. Feedback: correct, one more primary report reaches four; epoch count is not the counter. This simpler report-only response followed explicit tutoring and does not demonstrate independent epoch-to-interval transfer. The original score remains 4/5.

Prompt: You maximize validation accuracy with a Bandit policy and slack_amount=0.10. At an eligible evaluation, the best trial has accuracy 0.88. Another trial's best reported accuracy is 0.80. What happens to that other trial? Select one answer.
- A. It is terminated — Its accuracy is below the allowed threshold.
- B. The sweep ends — Its result stops every trial.
- C. It continues — Its accuracy is within the allowed margin.
- D. It must reach 0.98 — The required accuracy is best plus slack.

Initial C; key C; correct. Explanation: threshold 0.88 − 0.10 = 0.78; 0.80 is acceptable. A applies below threshold, B confuses trial and sweep stopping, D adds instead of subtracting slack for maximization. Follow-up: none.
